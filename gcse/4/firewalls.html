A firewall is a network security system that acts as a barrier between an organisation's internal network and the internet. It inspects the data traffic going in and out, and uses a set of rules to decide what to let through.

Set the rules

You are in charge of this company's firewall. Switch on the rules you want, then run the traffic through it. The aim is to stop every threat without getting in the way of people doing their jobs.

Internetuntrusted

The organisation that owns a firewall customises the rules to suit its own circumstances. Rules that are too loose let threats through. Rules that are too strict stop the network being useful.

What firewall rules can do

A rule can…Why
Stop certain protocols being used, such as FTPTo prevent the organisation's data being copied to an external server
Block data coming from or going to certain network addressesThe addresses could be particular computers, servers or websites, or a whole range of addresses belonging to particular countries or organisations
Disallow data that matches the pattern an attacker would useTo stop attempts at hacking the internal network's servers
Control what insiders doTo prevent people downloading viruses, emailing sensitive data outside the organisation, visiting harmful or time-wasting websites, or downloading from file sharing sites

Firewalls also flag up suspicious activity taking place on the internal network.
