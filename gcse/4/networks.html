A network is two or more computers connected together so that they can share resources and data. Desktops, laptops, phones, games consoles, shop tills and Internet of Things devices can all be linked in a network. Almost every organisation has one.

What a network lets you do

This small office has three computers, one printer and one file server. Choose a task and compare what happens with and without a network.

Networks also make it easier to look after the computers. Backups, software updates, security and remote help can all be run centrally by IT support, instead of visiting every machine.

LANs and WANs

Networks are described by their reach: how big an area they cover.

Local area network (LAN)Wide area network (WAN)
Area coveredA relatively small area, often a single site such as a home, a school, a hospital or a factoryA large geographical area, spanning many sites, cities or countries
What it connectsComputers, printers, routers and other devicesLANs to other LANs
Connected byCable or wirelessA variety of transmission media: cables, fibre optic links, satellites, microwaves
Who owns itTypically a single organisation or household, which owns all the equipmentThe links between sites are owned and maintained by telecommunication companies, who charge a fee to use them
ExampleA school network, a home Wi-Fi networkA bank linking its branches. The internet is the world's largest public WAN

A WAN lets an organisation keep its data synchronised across every site, send out software and updates easily, and avoid duplicating jobs such as IT support at each location. Staff can log in and work together wherever they are.
