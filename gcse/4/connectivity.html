Devices on a network are connected by a transmission medium. It can be wired, using copper or fibre optic cable, or wireless, using radio waves. The choice affects how fast, how far and how reliably data can be sent.

Three ways to carry data

Copper cableData travels as electrical pulses. Unshielded twisted pair (UTP) is the most common cable in a LAN: pairs of thin copper wires twisted together to reduce interference.
Fibre optic cableData travels as pulses of light along very thin strands of glass. It is not affected by electrical interference.
Wireless (Wi-Fi)Data travels as radio waves, with no physical connection. Each device has a wireless adapter and an antenna.

Many networks use both. Portable devices connect wirelessly, while equipment that stays put and needs a fast, steady connection, such as servers, desktop PCs and printers, is cabled.

Other wireless technologies

Wi-Fi is not the only wireless technology. These low-power alternatives send data over a short range.

BluetoothUp to about 10 metresLinking two nearby devices, such as a wireless mouse and a laptop, or earbuds and a phone
