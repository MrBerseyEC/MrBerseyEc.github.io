A protocol is a set of rules that governs how devices communicate. Devices made by different manufacturers, running different software, can exchange data because they all follow the same agreed rules about how it is formatted, addressed, sent and checked.

Why rules are needed

The sender turns each character into 8 bits. The receiver gets nothing but a stream of 1s and 0s. Whether it makes sense of them depends on whether it follows the same rules as the sender.

POP3 or IMAP?

Both collect email from a mail server, but they treat the mailbox differently. Alex reads her email on a phone and on a laptop. Check the mail on one device and then the other, with each protocol.

With IMAP the email stays on the server and each device shows a synchronised view of it, so IMAP suits someone who uses several devices. POP3 downloads the messages and normally removes them from the server, which frees up space there and lets you read them offline, but only on that one device.

HTTP or HTTPS?

You log in to a website from a café's public Wi-Fi. Someone at the next table is capturing the wireless traffic. This is what they see.

HTTPS encrypts everything sent between the browser and the web server. It should always be used when personal or financial data is involved, and most websites now use it for everything.
