The smallest unit of data is the bit. Bits are grouped into bytes, and bytes are counted in binary multiples: each unit is 1024 times the one before, because 1024 is 210. You need these units to work out file sizes and to decide whether something will fit on a storage device.

You will also see kilobyte, megabyte and gigabyte. Those prefixes strictly mean 1000, not 1024, which has caused a lot of confusion. The binary prefixes kibi, mebi, gibi and tebi were introduced to make it clear that the multiple is 1024. They are the ones to use in this course.

Converting between units

Moving to a smaller unit, there will be more of them, so multiply. Moving to a larger unit, there will be fewer, so divide. Do one step of the ladder at a time.

How many will fit?

To find out how many files fit on a device, put both sizes into the same unit and divide. Any part of a file left over does not count.
