Decomposition means breaking a big problem down into smaller parts that are each easier to solve. "Make a game" is too big to start on. "Make a duck that moves with the arrow keys" is something you can do in two blocks. In MakeCode Arcade, each part usually becomes its own event or its own function.

Break a big problem down

The whole problem is at the top. Click a dashed box to break it into smaller parts. Keep going until every part is small enough to build in a few blocks. Try to say what the parts will be before you click.

The boxes at the bottom are the jobs you actually build. Each one can be made and tested on its own, and different people can work on different boxes at the same time.

Build Duck Dash one part at a time

Duck Dash has been decomposed into five parts. Build them one at a time, and press Check after each one before you move on. A good way to keep each part separate is to put it in its own function, then call it from on start. The on switch on each event or function lets you turn a part off to test the others without it. If you get stuck, Show me adds that part for you.

    Find the bug by switching parts off

    This version of Duck Dash has a bug: the game ends after 3 seconds, when it should last 30. Because the game is made of separate parts, you can switch parts off one at a time and run it again. When switching a part off makes the bug go away, the bug is in that part.

    One big lump, or named parts?

    Here are two programs that make exactly the same game. One is a single lump. The other is decomposed into parts with names. Choose a change, then click the block you would need to edit. Try it in both versions.

    Change to make

    In the lump, you have to read every block until you find the right one. With named parts, the names tell you where to look, so you can ignore everything else. The game works the same either way. The difference is how easy it is for a person to understand and change.

    Share the work

    Decomposing a game means a team can build it together. Choose who builds each part and see how long the project takes. A team has to spend 5 minutes at the start agreeing on names, such as "the duck is called mySprite and is of kind Player", or the parts will not fit together.

    Plan your own game

    Think of a game. Break it down into up to six parts and give each part a short name, such as make the player or spawn an enemy. Then turn your plan into blocks: each part becomes an empty function, ready for you to fill in and test one at a time. Or load the example to see a finished space shooter built this way.
