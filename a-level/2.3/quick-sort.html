A quick sort is also a divide and conquer algorithm. It picks an item called the pivot and rearranges the list so that smaller items are on one side of it and larger items are on the other. The pivot is then in its final place, and the same method is applied to each side.

How it works

  1. If the list has fewer than two items it is already sorted. Stop. This is the base case.
  2. Choose a pivot. In this demo it is the last item.
  3. Partition the list. Work along it, and every item that is less than or equal to the pivot is moved into a group at the left. Items larger than the pivot are left on the right.
  4. Swap the pivot into the gap between the two groups. It is now in its final position.
  5. Quick sort the items to the left of the pivot, then quick sort the items to the right of it.
  6. There is no merge step. When every call has finished, the list is sorted.
  • It sorts in place, using swaps within the list, so it needs no second list.
  • The pivot choice matters. A pivot near the middle value splits the list evenly. A pivot that is the smallest or largest value splits off just one item.
  • Choosing the last item as pivot is simple but performs badly on a list that is already sorted. Choosing a random item, or the median of the first, middle and last items, avoids this.
  • It is not stable: swaps can change the order of equal items.
